# Response to VrBX — working draft

**Draft, not submitted.** Based on the original paper and 8 November notes. E01 results are not incorporated yet. Validate all numbers before submission.

[Concern checklist](../common/ISSUES.md#reviewer-vrbx) · [Shared drafting notes](../common/response-notes.md)

**V1 — Exact policy versus implemented sampler.** You are correct that the two require different validation. Equation (13) describes the selected exact-equilibrium policy under Appendix A.3. At an exact solution, the endpoint reconstructs the base noise through the inverse step relations, making the selected map one-to-one under those assumptions. In practice, finite stopping, matrix shifts, and clipping modify the calculation. Table 3 compares final-checkpoint actions, safeguarded density readouts, and actor gradients against an independently solved implicit reference. It does not establish the likelihood or full parameter sensitivity of the actual finite-iteration program. Appendix C.1 explicitly leaves finite-sampler likelihood and errors during learning open. The conditional equilibrium formula and its approximate implementation must therefore be assessed at their respective levels of evidence.

**V2 — PushWall and final evaluation.** PushWall remains the clear exception in the reported median/P95 audit. Tightening reduces its median gradient discrepancy from 13.7% to 2.84%, but P95 remains 50.3%, versus 96.1% originally. Its tighter P95 safeguarded density discrepancy is 0.197 nats. Three inputs without a validated reference are excluded. On the other four tasks, the original P95 gradient discrepancies are at most 0.836% and safeguarded density discrepancies at most 0.00191 nats. Tighter P95 gradient discrepancies are below 0.002%. These quantiles do not bound every error or establish uniform improvement. Table 4 changes final sampled success by at most 0.33 percentage points, but this measures evaluation sensitivity. PushWall's unchanged 0.3% success is near the floor and cannot establish harmless training error.

**V3 — Representative training checkpoints.** Table 3 supplies evidence about approximation to the equilibrium calculation on the audited final-policy inputs. Extending that evidence to early/intermediate checkpoints requires distinguishing production gradients, finite-program sensitivities and a branch-matched equilibrium reference on matched inputs. Effects on the actor objective and entropy-dependent learning signals also matter. Those measurements are absent from the submitted results. A learning intervention would answer the separate question of cumulative sensitivity to solver settings. Re-evaluating a frozen policy cannot establish that causal learning effect.

**V4 — Uncertainty and depth.** The docking result is an observed mean advantage. For wide-tight docking, Table 1 reports U4 of 0.589 ± 0.128 for DEFlow and 0.502 ± 0.011 for DIME, as mean ± sample SD. U4 measures successful goal coverage, not episode success percentage. PushWall's return average, 217.8 ± 334.9, also has substantial seed variation. More evaluation episodes do not replace independent training seeds. The wide-tight explicit aggregate contains only two completed runs, so its sample count and failed-run exclusions must remain visible. Appendix C.3 is one surviving checkpoint pair, with original explicit seeds 1 and 2 failing numerically. It illustrates behavior of that pair, not general depth robustness. DEFlow itself loses accuracy at two steps in this illustration.

**V5 — Elapsed time and costs.** The current experiments do not answer whether the advantage persists at equal wall-clock budgets against SAC-Flow and DIME. Section 6 reports approximate costs of three times SAC's training time and about 40% lower throughput than SAC-Flow, with costs of the same order as DIME and a shared-hardware caveat. These are not controlled runtime comparisons. Hardware, field-point evaluations, and nonlinear/dense-backward costs need explicit accounting for that purpose. Manipulation “time-averaged return” is implemented as normalized trapezoidal integration of return over recorded training steps, not elapsed time or a simple arithmetic mean of checkpoint values. The manipulation curves compare the evaluated recipes at recorded step counts. They are not an isolated sampler comparison or a controlled elapsed-time comparison. Return-versus-time curves require valid timing records rather than relabeling their horizontal axes.
